# Varāha in *Viṣṇu Temples of Kāñchipuram* — preface and ch4

Companion note to [`varaha_in_vimanarcanakalpa.md`](varaha_in_vimanarcanakalpa.md).
Source: R. Nagaswamy, *Viṣṇu Temples of Kāñchipuram*, Tamil Arts Academy,
2nd edn. 2025 (preface dated 1 January 2011) —
`../Vishnu_Temples_Of_Kanchi/generate_epub/OPF/preface.html` and

## 1. Preface

Two references only.

1. **Frontispiece.** The book opens on a full-width Varāha plate —
   `images/Front_Varaha.png`, `id="varaha"` — paired with a half-width
   `Front_Devotee.png`. Varāha is thus the first image in the volume, before the
   preface text begins.
2. **Ādi-Varāha as a *divya deśa*.** Of the 108 *divya deśas* of Tamiḻnāḍu sung by
   the Āḻvārs, fourteen are in Kāñchipuram. Two of those fourteen sit **inside
   Śivakṣetras**: Nilattiṅgaḷ Tuṇḍam within the Ekāmranātha temple, and
   **Ādi-Varāha within the Kāmākṣi temple**. (Four more share the Ūraham /
   Ulakaḷanda-Perumāḷ complex.)

The preface says nothing further about Varāha; its argument is about factual
versus legendary temple history, the merchant guilds' role in temple
administration, and Varadarāja's late (12th-century, Cōḻa) integration into the
city.

## 2. Chapter 4 — two distinct Varāha sculptures

The Vaikuṇṭa Perumāḷ temple is, on Nagaswamy's argument, the only early temple in
Tamiḻnāḍu where both the *vyūha* and *vibhava* forms are carved on the main
sanctum walls. Varāha appears **twice**, in two different aspects.

### 2.1 Bhūvarāha — south wall, ground floor (fig. 4.33)

Part of the anticlockwise *daśāvatāra* sequence. Nagaswamy's description:

> The Varāha is shown next as if springing from the depth of the ocean with the
> right leg kicking up forcefully get to the top of the waters while supporting
> the Goddess earth with his left leg who is shown seated on his thigh. With his
> right hand the Varāha is supporting the legs of the goddess while with the left
> he is embracing her. In this pose he has turned his face towards the goddess
> with affection. The goddes full of love and facing the God holds her hand in
> añjali pose. By the side of the Varāha is shown the Ananta who is shown in
> añjali hasta in supplication.

Caption to fig. 4.33: Varāha emerging from the deep oceanic waters lifting
Bhūdevī; the multi-hooded snake **Ananta** below the left leg of Varāha; south
wall of the sanctum tower, Pallava, 8th century.

### 2.2 Yajña Varāha — north wall, ground floor (figs. 4.18, 4.21)

Standing, four hands: *cakra* and *śaṅkha* in the rear arms; front right in
*cin-mudrā*, front left in *kaṭi-hasta*. Before him stands another four-armed
figure — right arm in a pointing pose, left broken, **apparently three-headed**.

Nagaswamy offers **two readings of that attendant figure in two different
sections**, without reconciling them:

- §4.4 — "Possibly, this figure might represent **Śiva** to whom the Yajñamūrti is
  imparting knowledge."
- §4.6 — "the Varāha portrayed here is not the Varāha that uplifted the earth, but
  as the giver of knowledge, in which aspect he is said to teach the sage
  **Sanatkumāra** the Purāṇas and the knowledge of the Vaiṣṇava faith. Sanatkumāra
  occupies a special position in Vaiṣṇava theology, and probably this event is
  depicted here."

§4.3 notes generally that "there seems to be some emphasis on the Varāha form" in
the sanctum-wall programme.

### 2.3 A figure-numbering slip

§4.3 keys the remark about emphasis on the Varāha form to **fig. 4.19**, but
fig. 4.19 is Kalki/Hayagrīva; the Yajña Varāha plates are **4.18** and **4.21**.
Worth correcting if this text is ever edited.

## 3. Varāha in the lists ch4 cites

### 3.1 The temple's own *daśāvatāra* sequence (§4.4.3)

Carved anticlockwise from the south-west corner of the main wall, called the
*vibhava* forms: Matsya, Kūrma, **Varāha**, Narasiṁha, Vāmana (Trivikrama),
Paraśurāma, Balarāma, Dāśarati-rāma, Kṛṣṇa, Kalki. Varāha is **third** — the same
position it holds in Vimānārcanā-kalpa ch21 and ch55.

### 3.2 The Saṁkarṣaṇa-Saṁhitā's 35 *vibhavas* (§4.5)

The Saṁkarṣaṇa-Saṁhitā (also called Nārada-Pāñcarātra-Saṁhitā) gives 35 *vibhava*
manifestations rather than ten. Two entries in that list are boar forms:
**Kroḍātmā** (10th) and **Varāha** (16th).

Nagaswamy's argument: the list carries Varāha, Vāmana and Narasiṁha **twice each**,
and the temple matches this by portraying each twice —

| Form | First portrayal | Second portrayal |
|---|---|---|
| Varāha | **Bhūvarāha**, south, ground floor | **Yajña Varāha** the teacher, north, ground floor |
| Vāmana | Kevala Vāmana, south, ground floor | Vāmana within the Trivikrama episode, upper floor |
| Narasiṁha | Nṛhari standing alone, south, ground floor | fighting Hiraṇya, and ripping him — two upper-floor panels |

He does not name which two list-entries constitute the Varāha pair. *Kroḍātmā*
(*kroḍa* = boar) and *Varāha* are the obvious candidates — an inference, not
something the text states.

On this basis he concludes the temple follows the **Pāñcarātra school of the
Saṁkarṣaṇa-Saṁhitā**, arguing against Dennis Hudson's use of the Bhāgavata-Purāṇa
as the iconographic key (Hudson, *The Body of God*, posthumous, 2008) on the
grounds that the Purāṇa postdates the temple.

### 3.3 The Māmallapuram Ādivarāha cave inscription (§4.6)

Cited as roughly 25 years later than the Vaikuṇṭa Perumāḷ Kalki figure. Its ten
avatāras: Matsya, Kūrma, **Varāha**, Narasiṁha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Balarāma,
Kodaṇḍarāma, **Buddha**, Kalki — omitting Kṛṣṇa, on Nagaswamy's reading because
Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Being who *was born as* the ten. The Vaikuṇṭa Perumāḷ
programme takes the other option: Kṛṣṇa as Govardhanadhāri, no Buddha.

## 4. Varāha as Pallava dynastic allegory (§4.9.4)

In the ground-floor *prākāra* panels narrating Pallava history, the penance
sequence carries an allusion to the **Varāha avatāra in the act of lifting
Bhūmidevī**: the Pallava race resembling an incarnation of Viṣṇu in conquering the
world, and purifying it as the Ganges does.

## 5. Varāha in the Prabandham (§4.20)

**Thirumaṅgai Āḻvār** sings Parameśvara Viṇṇagaram in ten verses. Listing Kāñci's
manifestations of Viṣṇu, he says that "as an animal he entered the sea and
bestowed grace" — i.e. as Varāha — between Anantaśāyi creating the universe from
the navel-lotus and Kṛṣṇa swallowing the universe.

## 6. Comparison with Vimānārcanā-kalpa ch56

The **Bhūvarāha** panel tracks Ādi-Varāha's *lakṣaṇa* in ch56 closely: the goddess
borne on the thigh, one hand supporting her feet and the other embracing her, the
face turned to her, her hands in *añjali*/*prāñjali*, the serpent king present at
the foot, and the Lord rising up out of the waters/*rasātala*.

Divergences:

| | Vimānārcanā-kalpa ch56 (Ādi-Varāha) | Vaikuṇṭa Perumāḷ south wall |
|---|---|---|
| Serpent | right foot **planted on** Śeṣa's hood-gem; left leg bent | Ananta **below the left leg**, in *añjali*, as supplicant |
| Legs | right foot down, left bent for the ascent | **right leg kicking up**; goddess on the **left** thigh |
| Face | *mukhena devīṃ jighrantaṃ* — sniffs/kisses her | turns his face toward her "with affection" |
| Goddess's mood | *vrīḍā-harṣa-saṃyuktā* — bashfulness and joy | "full of love and facing the God" |

The **Yajña Varāha** panel matches ch56's Yajña-Varāha only in name. Ch56 has him
*seated* on a *siṃhāsana*, white, in yellow garments, flanked by Śrī and Mahī. The
Kāñchi figure is **standing**, alone, in *cin-mudrā* — a teaching aspect ch56 does
not describe at all. The teaching pupil is also of interest: ch56 places
**Sanaka–Sanatkumāra** in Varāha's retinue in the *nava-mūrti* scheme (ch36), which
supports Nagaswamy's §4.6 reading of the attendant as Sanatkumāra over his §4.4
suggestion of Śiva.
